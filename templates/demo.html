Three ways to put the dive on a page

The same story and the same files, shown as a window inside an article, as a section the reader scrolls through, and as an iframe for sites that cannot run scripts.

1. A window in the page

The player keeps to its box. Press Play, drag sideways to scrub, or tap the dots. Once you have pressed or clicked it, the mouse wheel scrubs it too, and hands the scroll back to the page at either end, so readers who only pass by are never caught. More opens the full text while it keeps moving; Pause eases to a stop on the next scene.

Size it like an image: it takes the width it is given and keeps the story's 16:9 shape, or set a height in CSS. Colours and fonts come from the manifest; the page can still override any --dt-* variable.

2. A section the reader scrolls through

With mode="scroll" the dive pins to the screen while the reader scrolls past it, then lets the page carry on. Play still works, and the page lands where the story stopped.

Scroll down into the story.

3. An iframe

For a CMS that strips scripts, embed the hosted player page. It plays muted on its own when it scrolls into view with ?autoplay=1.

Options

AttributeMeaning
srcThe story's manifest.json. Layer files are found next to it.
modeplayer (default), scroll, or page for a whole page.
chromefull adds the brand bar and its button; minimal (default outside page mode) keeps only sound and full screen.
autoplayPlayer only: plays muted while at least half of it is in view, until the reader takes over.

Script control: el.play(), el.pause(), el.restart(), el.goTo(i). Events: dt-ready, dt-scene, dt-play, dt-pause, dt-end.
